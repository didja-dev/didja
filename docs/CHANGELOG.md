## 2026-11-14

### Day Review and a morning list, with Not Done on every tick; the first tick says so once (SCR-100)
- **Day Review**, off unless you choose a time (Settings › General): a short sheet at the top of the Quick View with what Didja ticked for you today (each with why and **Not Done**), Waiting On items answered today, what is still open from today and what is due tomorrow. A **Morning** list at its own time: due today, overdue, waiting on. Also from "…" › Tasks › Day Review / Morning List, and `didja review`.
- **Not Done** reopens a tick: it records the tick as wrong (and the place as not trusted) as reopening it anywhere does, and for a tick from what a screen showed it also teaches "looks done" (`notDone`).
- The first time Didja ever ticks something for you, the Quick View shows it once with why and **Undo** (this was left for a follow-up in SCR-74).
- Engine: `engine/src/review.ts` (`buildReview`, `reviewMarkdown`, `dueReview`, `undoTick`, `firstTickNote`); store `tickReason`, `firstTick`; CLI `didja review [evening|morning] [--json|--markdown] | due [--take] | time | times | undo | first-tick [seen]`. - Tests: `engine/test/review.test.ts` (6): the evening sections from fixtures (a command tick and an accepted "looks done" listed with why; a step you ticked yourself not listed; a waiting item answered today; open from today; due tomorrow); the morning sections; Not Done reopens and records `reopened-after-tick`, teaches the accepted one, and refuses a task Didja did not tick; the Markdown; off by default, once a day, not the morning list after noon; the first tick shown once. ### Waiting On: asks you sent, until that person answers (SCR-99)
- An ask in your own chat message or sent email ("Can you send me the signed contract?", "Sam, could you review the PR?", "תוכל לשלוח את החוזה?") is kept under **Waiting On**, a folded tray with a count under the Quick View's list: "Waiting on Dana: the signed contract". Its own table (schema 032 `waiting`), never a task: not ticked, not counted. One-to-one chats wait on the other person; groups only on the person named first; email only when sent to one person. Read by wording only (`engine/src/waiting.ts`): a plain ask with something concrete.
- Quiet for a day after a chat ask and two working days after an email (Settings › General, `didja waiting quiet`), then the row says **no reply yet** and one reminder ("No reply yet · Waiting on Dana: …"). It closes as answered on a message from that person in the same chat or email conversation after the ask; **Got it** and **×** (Stop waiting) close it by hand. `didja waiting [--json] | gotit | stop | why`; `why` says how it closed. Closed rows are deleted after a day, open ones 30 days after the ask.
- Tests: `engine/test/waiting.test.ts` (10): wording in English and Hebrew and lines that are not asks; quiet times (an email on Friday is quiet until Tuesday); a chat ask waits and makes no task; your own follow-up, their reaction and their message from before the ask do not close it, their reply after it does, with who and when, and no task or tick; in a group only the named person closes it and an ask naming no one waits on no one; already answered on screen makes nothing; one reminder after the quiet time, Got it and Stop close without a tick, pruning; the setting; an email to one person closes only on their reply, not on someone else's in the thread; an email to several people, or someone else's ask, waits on no one. Swift `WaitingTests` (5): the tray is read from the table (answered rows left out), "no reply yet" only after the quiet time, the reminder's words and its own notification id, the quiet-time setting; the tray render (looked at). Schema 32 bundled.
- **Not built:** the Full View does not show Waiting On (the Quick View does); no model reads asks (wording only). **Not verified:** on live chats and mail, and the live reminder.

### What you said you'd do: promises in your own messages (SCR-98)
- A promise in your own chat message or sent email ("I'll send the slides tomorrow", "I will get back to you on the quote by Friday", "אשלח את הקובץ מחר") becomes a task, "You said you'd: send the slides" ("הבטחת: …" in Hebrew), under that chat or mailbox, with its due day. Read by wording only (`engine/src/promises.ts`): first person, future, not hedged, not a question, with something concrete to do. Vaguer chat promises go to Suggested (not under Strict); vaguer email promises make nothing. A promise that answers someone's ask makes nothing (the ask's task stands for it).
- Never ticked: a later message or email of yours there that hands something over asks "looks done?"; saying it again does nothing; a promise is left out of every screen-watching path. Not an Action remembers its wording; Accept on a suggested promise makes a promise task.
- Benchmark: three made-up scenarios (100–102): a promise said again, "here you go" with nothing attached, and a promise to someone in a group next to their own. Scenario files now sort by number, so 100 follows 99. No wrong ticks; the "here you go" scenario counts one wrong ask (it asks "looks done?").
- Tests: `engine/test/promises.test.ts` (10): wording in English and Hebrew, sure and vague, and lines that are not promises; a chat promise with its source, reason and due day, not watched on other screens; restated, one task, not done; a message that hands nothing over does nothing and "here you go" only asks, even with a reply judge that would settle; a promise answering an ask makes no second task; a group promise is yours and someone else's is not; vague goes to Suggested (none under Strict), Accept makes a promise task; Not an Action keeps the same wording out; an email promise from your own words only (not the quoted message), with "Attached: q3-slides.pdf" only asking; someone else's email promise is not yours.
- **Not built:** the model judging promises (wording only); a promise named in the target where it is done (it asks). **Not verified:** on live chats and mail.

### Why a task was made, kept from the moment it is captured (SCR-96)
- Every new task keeps one engine-written line saying why it is a task (schema 030 `tasks.captured_why`): a list handed to you by its wording, one the model judged yours, one no model could judge, one you settled by ticking a step, a command step, an email asking for a reply or an action, a chat ask (one-to-one, tagged or named, to every member, accepted from Suggested), your own tasks, a connection's item, a follow-up Didja added.
- `didja why` starts with "Made: <why> · <how sure> · captured <when> from <where>"; the band (sure, possibly yours, checking whose it is) follows the task's confidence, so it changes when the model decides. The Full View's detail pane shows it (it shows `why`), and the Quick View shows the reason and band when you point at a row's title.
- Reasons hold no screen text and no person's name: only the kind of source, the AI tool's or connection's name and how it was decided.
- Tests: `engine/test/capturewhy.test.ts` (5): lists by wording with a command step; checking, then judged; no model; you ticked one while checking; email, the four chat paths, an accepted suggestion, your own and My List tasks, each with its reason and none holding a name or the message's words; the default by source and the bands; and on every benchmark scenario every captured task has a reason and none holds a line from the screen or a task's title. Swift: schema 30 bundled; a row's hover text (reason and band, none on older tasks).
- **Not verified:** the hover text in the live app. Tasks captured before this version have no reason; nothing is back-filled.

### Opt-in agent hooks: Claude Code, Cursor and Windsurf tell Didja what they ran and planned (SCR-88)
- Settings › General › **Assistants**: one switch per tool, off by default, each saying exactly what it adds. On puts an entry in the tool's user-level hooks file (Claude Code `~/.claude/settings.json`: PostToolUse for Bash and TodoWrite; Cursor `~/.cursor/hooks.json`: afterShellExecution; Windsurf `~/.codeium/windsurf/hooks.json`: post_run_command), never a repository's, after copying the file to `.didja-backup`. It runs the app's engine as `didja hook <tool> <event>`, which keeps only the tool, event, time, a command line and its exit code, and plan item titles, 24 hours (`agent-hooks.jsonl`); never file contents, prompts, replies, output or transcripts. Off removes only our entry and forgets what that tool said.
- A step from that tool whose command it then ran (after the step was made), or that is one of its own plan items, is taken back with why ("Claude Code ran this itself (its hook said so)"). Nothing from a hook ticks a task; a step from another source (the same command asked in the Claude app) is not touched.
- `didja agent-hooks on|off <tool>|status [--json]`; Privacy lists it ("What your AI tools tell Didja"); CLAUDE.md lists it as the maintainer's third exception besides Connections.
- Tests: `engine/test/agenthooks.test.ts` (10): per tool, on adds our entry once and keeps everything else (made-up files with other hooks and permissions), backs the file up, off restores it exactly; each tool's shape; one tool's entries are never another's; a file that is not JSON is left alone; only the listed fields are kept from made-up events full of other fields (a Read or Edit keeps nothing); 24 hours; a ran command and a plan item take back that tool's steps, never tick, and leave the Claude app's same step alone; an earlier run, another tool or no log change nothing; `didja hook` keeps one line of only those fields, prints nothing, exits 0 on bad input, and never opens the database. Swift: the switches' status and words. Looked at the Settings render. Benchmark unchanged, wrong ticks 0.
- **Not built:** Copilot in VS Code: the issue names its hooks as `.github/hooks/*.json`, inside each repository, and the issue rules out writing a repository's files. **Not verified:** the real tools running these hooks; the files' shapes and the events' fields follow each tool's documented hooks format as far as known (exit codes: none of the three is known to send one, so none is usually kept); edits (Edit/Write) are not recorded, as file paths are not among the fields the issue allows.

### Commands you run: exact exit codes with an opt-in shell hook, failures read from more prompts, and editor terminals (SCR-87)
- **Shell hook** (Settings › General › Commands, `didja shell-hook on|off|status`; off by default): adds marked lines to ~/.zshrc and ~/.bashrc and a fish function that write each command you run, its exit code and a hash of its folder to `shell.log` beside Didja's data, kept 24 hours; key- or token-looking commands only as a hash. A command step ticks on an exact run after it was made that ended with exit 0, in any terminal; never on a failure, nor on a run before the step; when every exact run since the step failed, a screen where it looked fine does not tick it. Turning it off removes exactly those lines, the fish function and the log. Listed in Privacy ("Commands you run").
- **Failures from the prompt:** a powerlevel10k "✘ 1" on the right of the next prompt was read as a command typed there, so the run before it counted as fine (a wrong tick); fish's "~/repo [1]>" was not recognised as the next prompt. Both now mean the command before failed.
- **Editor terminals:** `didja doctor` and Help › Troubleshooting Report say when VS Code, Cursor or Windsurf was read but never its built-in terminal (drawn as a picture) while a command step is open, and suggest the shell hook or the editor's `editor.accessibilitySupport` (Didja never changes the editor's settings); likewise when Warp gives no text.
- Codex's "✓ • 5ms" / "✗ (N)" are its own runs: SCR-85 takes those steps back as the agent's, and agent windows never tick.
- Tests: `engine/test/shellhook.test.ts` (11): install adds one block, to .bashrc only if there is one, fish only if set up, twice changes nothing; remove takes out exactly the block, the fish function and the log, keeping your lines (a file with no final newline gains one); a damaged block is left alone; the zsh and bash lines, run in a made-up home, write the command and exit code and a key-looking command only as a hash; the log keeps 24 hours; exact run with exit 0 ticks with why; a failure, an earlier run, another command or a hashed line never ticks; a failure in the log vetoes a screen that looked fine; with the hook off nothing is read; p10k's "✘ 1" / "✘ 127" and fish's "[1]" mean failed, "✔" and a plain prompt fine. `doctor.test.ts`: the editor finding and its absence once its terminal was read. Swift: the switch's status and words. Looked at the Settings and Privacy renders. Benchmark unchanged (no scenario uses the hook): wrong ticks 0.
- **Not verified:** the hook in a real interactive zsh, bash or fish session (the lines were run in non-interactive shells, with the hooks called by hand; fish not run at all); turning it on from Settings on a real Mac (it would edit your ~/.zshrc; not done here); Warp's and the editors' findings on live readings. The SCR-82 "command ticks up" benchmark needs scenarios with a shell log, not added.

### A live chat is one task, and only if you leave it unanswered (SCR-45)
- A real case: a 20-minute back-and-forth in a WhatsApp direct chat made 10 open tasks, one per message, then marked most of them "looks done" one by one; one was a statement, not an ask, and some were worded "Tell <name> what he thinks".
- An ask now waits 10 minutes for a reply of yours in that chat; if you answer while the conversation goes on, it never becomes a task. If you don't, it becomes one after 10 minutes, even when the chat is no longer on screen (schema 018, `chat_pending`, keeps only the model's wording of it meanwhile).
- One task per conversation: further asks in the same chat join its open task, which is worded after the newest ask with "· +N", and one reply of yours settles them together (one "looks done" to confirm, not one per message). A new ask clears a "looks done" the task was showing.
- Chats that already have several open tasks are folded into one per chat, once, the next time the engine runs.
- The chat instructions: a statement about the sender (plans, wishes, offers) or something already answered is not an ask; a question is worded "Answer <name>: <the question>" (Hebrew: "ענה ל<name>: …"); vague wording such as "what he thinks" is refused.
- Tests: engine 235 (a live reply makes no task, 10 minutes without one makes one, several asks are one task and one reply settles it, another chat's reply or a reply that puts it off answers nothing, a new ask reopens the question, the one-time fold) and the app's chat instructions. ### Go to Source: find the window by what is in it, and scroll after reopening a page
- It did not scroll anywhere: it looked for the window by its title, and the titles rarely match. Claude's window is titled "Claude" while capture names it after the conversation, and a browser window's title follows whichever tab is in front. It then gave up and reopened the address. Now it searches each of the app's windows for the line (the one with the matching title first), raises the one that has it and scrolls there. If no window has the line, a browser reopens the page, waits up to 6 seconds for it to load, and then scrolls.
- In Claude (and other apps with a sidebar of conversations), when the line is not in the open conversation, the conversation is opened from the sidebar by its name, then scrolled to. Not verified live: in the run tried, the conversation was already open, so the sidebar step did not happen.
- Outlook for Mac (and other apps with long native lists): if no window shows the email, it pages down each message list, up to 30 times, until the subject turns up. Then it selects the row, which opens the email, and scrolls to it. A list that does not have it is scrolled back to the top. Not verified live: on the task tried, the email was not among the 51 rows the Inbox list holds and the list did not load more, so only the window was raised.
- `ScrolledAway --go-to-source <place.json>` runs it from a terminal and prints each step (counts and sizes only, never screen text).
- Verified live in Edge on an Outlook on the web task: the email was reopened and scrolled to after about a second. A Claude task was found and scrolled to in its open conversation. Not tried: terminals, Outlook for Mac.

### Go to Source
- Right-click a task and choose **Go to Source**, press **G** on a highlighted row, or use the button in the task window. It brings the app forward, raises the window the task was captured in, reads that window again to find the line (by the task's words, or an email's subject) and scrolls it into view. If the window has gone it reopens the page's address in the browser it came from, or else just brings the app forward. Nothing read while looking is kept, and the exclude list and password fields are handled the same way as during capture.
- Schema 14 keeps the app, window title and page address on each task, so this still works after the observation it came from has expired. Tasks captured earlier get theirs from their first sighting, while that sighting is still kept.
- Not verified in the live app: no jump was tried on screen. Tests cover the matching, the window lookup and the engine's `place` command. Scrolling depends on the app honouring `AXScrollToVisible`; terminals and chats that load lazily may only raise the window.

### Fix: the app crashed reading a very deep window
- Reading a window's Accessibility tree recurses once per level, up to 100 levels. On the capture queue's thread (512 KB of stack) a deep page overflowed the stack and the app quit (crash report 2026-11-10 23:58, inside `AXCapture.Walk.node`). The walk now runs on its own thread with a 16 MB stack. A test checks the walk gets the large stack even when called from a dispatch queue. Not verified: the page that caused it is not known, so the crash itself was not reproduced.
